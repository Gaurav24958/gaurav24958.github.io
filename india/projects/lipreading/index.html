Executive Summary & Research Motivation

Lipreading — deciphering speech purely from visual mouth and lip dynamics — is a vital technology for hearing-impaired individuals, noisy industrial environments, and security surveillance where audio streams are corrupted or absent.

While visual speech recognition has made strides on Western corpora (e.g., GRID and LRW), existing models fail significantly when evaluated on Indian-accented English. Indian English exhibits distinct phonetic coarticulation, unique visemes, rapid syllable cadences, and diverse facial features. This project created a novel spatiotemporal deep learning framework explicitly engineered to address Indian-accented speech dynamics.

The Middle Processing Stage (Preprocessing Pipeline)

The video data preprocessing pipeline transforms unconstrained speaker video footage into normalized, spatiotemporal tensors optimized for deep neural network consumption.

Step 01 FFmpeg Utility

Format Conversion & Audio Stripping

Converts raw multi-format video files into standard MP4 containers. The audio track is completely removed to force the pipeline to rely exclusively on visual speech cues.

Why it matters: Eliminates auditory leakage and reduces storage and compute overhead across thousands of video tensors.
Step 02 Dlib Face Detector

Invariant Face Detection

Employs Dlib's facial detection framework to locate the speaker's face regardless of position, head tilt, or distance from the camera frame.

Why it matters: Anchors the pipeline to a rock-solid spatial reference point across varying speaker setups and environmental conditions.
Step 03 Dlib 68-Point Model

Lip Landmark Point Extraction

Extracts 68 facial landmark coordinates per frame, isolating key node points that delineate the outer lip boundary and inner mucosal opening.

Why it matters: Provides precise geometric coordinates of mouth deformations while discarding extraneous head, cheek, and eye motions.
Step 04 OpenCV ROI Cropping

ROI Cropping & Uniform Resizing (140 × 46 px)

Crops the Region of Interest (ROI) around the extracted lip landmark coordinates and resizes every frame uniformly to 140 × 46 pixels.

Why it matters: Removes background visual clutter, ensuring consistent dimensional tensors for convolutional filters without compute waste.
Step 05 NumPy Tensor Math

Color Standardization & Pixel Normalization

Standardizes RGB channels across all video frames to zero mean and unit variance (μ = 0, σ² = 1).

Why it matters: Neutralizes ambient room lighting shifts, shadows, and variations in skin tones across diverse Indian speakers.
Step 06 VSDC & .align Files

Spatiotemporal Sequencing (75 Frames @ 25 fps)

Packages 75 consecutive frames into unified tensor sequences (exact 3.0-second clips). Aligned frame-by-frame with word-level millisecond duration markers.

Why it matters: Preserves chronological syllable progression needed for recurrent sequence modeling and CTC loss alignment.

Dataset & Training Methodology

To address the lack of Indian-accented visual speech data, we synthesized a custom dataset of 500 video clips recorded across 15+ Indian participants articulating 20+ distinct sentences derived from the GRID corpus.

Each video was trimmed to exactly 3.0 seconds (75 frames at 25 fps) and synchronized on a frame-by-frame basis using VSDC Free Video Editor. The model was trained on 2,250 composite videos (custom Indian dataset + GRID dataset) for 100 epochs, reaching a validation loss of 3.043.

Deployment & Research Publications

Interactive Streamlit Web App: Deployed a user-friendly Streamlit interface that enables users to upload custom video files, runs automated landmark extraction and normalization in real-time, and outputs predicted sentence transcriptions alongside confidence scores.
