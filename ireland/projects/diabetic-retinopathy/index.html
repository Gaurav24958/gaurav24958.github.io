Executive Summary & Clinical Need

Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) is the primary cause of preventable blindness among working-age adults globally. Timely detection can prevent over 90% of severe vision loss, yet screening is severely bottlenecked by the shortage of trained ophthalmologists and specialized equipment in high-density or rural populations.

In the Deep Learning Lab at SCTR's PICT, we developed an automated computer vision screening model capable of grading retinopathy into 5 distinct clinical severity levels (No DR, Mild, Moderate, Severe, Proliferative DR) while detecting retinal swelling (macular edema). By combining morphological preprocessing with deep convolutional and recurrent architectures, the system achieved over 95% accuracy on sizable clinical datasets and was validated in hospital settings.

The Preprocessing & Medical Imaging Pipeline

Step 01 OpenCV Processing

Illumination Correction & Glare Removal

Applies Subtract Mean processing to correct non-uniform optical flash lighting and eliminate retinal reflection artifacts.

Why it matters: Standardizes illumination across fundus images taken on different hospital fundus camera hardware.
Step 02 CLAHE Filter

Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization

Locally enhances contrast in discrete contextual image tiles without amplifying background sensor noise.

Why it matters: Makes faint microaneurysms, hemorrhages, and neovascular vessels sharply prominent for feature extraction.
Step 03 Albumentations

Class-Balanced Augmentation

Employs affine shearing, elastic deformations, and weighted sampling to counter extreme medical class imbalances.

Why it matters: Prevents model bias toward common healthy retinas, ensuring high sensitivity for rare, vision-critical Proliferative DR.
Step 04 YOLOv8x + CNN

Multi-Scale Lesion Localization

Fine-tuned YOLOv8x deep backbone detects microaneurysms, hard exudates, cotton-wool spots, and macular swelling.

Why it matters: Generates interpretable visual heatmaps that provide clinical justification to ophthalmologists.
Step 05 LSTM Recurrent Units

Clinical Severity Grading

Integrates spatial lesion patterns into an ordinal classifier predicting disease stage and macular edema risk.

Why it matters: Directly translates computer vision features into clinical triage priorities for hospital workflows.

Clinical Validation & Impact

Our model was tested in real hospital settings against certified ophthalmologist readings, demonstrating consistent performance above 95% accuracy and outperforming previous automated baselines. The paper documenting this research is currently under consideration for publication in a Springer Journal.
